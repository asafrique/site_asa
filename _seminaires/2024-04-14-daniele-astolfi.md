The internal model principle for the linear robust output regulation problem was established in the 1970s. It states that, in order to perfectly track or reject disturbances robustly with respect to small model uncertainties, the controller needs to incorporate a copy of the model generating such signals. A typical well-known case is the use of integral action (e.g. PID regulators) to track or reject constant signals. In this talk, we revisit the general linear case, providing the main ideas and tools to design internal-model-based regulators. Then, some extensions are illustrated for nonlinear and PDE systems. Finally, engineering examples are shown, with particular relevance to harmonic regulation for the mechanical ventilation problem.
