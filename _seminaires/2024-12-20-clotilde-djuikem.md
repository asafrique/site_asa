Fungal diseases cause serious damage to crops worldwide. In particular, coffee leaf rust (CLR), caused by the fungus Hemileia vastatrix, attacks coffee leaves and reduces coffee yield. This paper presents a multi-seasonal model of CLR development in a coffee plantation, with continuous dynamics during the rainy season and a discrete event representing the simpler dynamics of the dry season. Biological control using hyperparasites, through one or more discrete introduction events over the year, is then added. Analytical and semi-numerical studies identify how much and how frequently hyperparasites need to be introduced, through the definition of a threshold value depending on various parameters. We show that the best control strategy depends on hyperparasite mortality: low-mortality parasites need be released only once a year, while high-mortality parasites should be released more frequently to ensure their persistence in the plantation. This work provides qualitative and quantitative bases for the deployment of hyperparasite-based biocontrol, a promising alternative to fungicides for rust control.
